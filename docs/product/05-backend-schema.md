# 5. Backend schema: where client data lives and who can see it

Last reviewed 9 October 2026. The source of truth is `drizzle/schema.ts` (PostgreSQL on Supabase, through Drizzle);
this document explains it. `test/docs/productDocs.test.ts` fails if a table is added to the schema without appearing in
section 2 or 3.

**The model in one line:** a **person** (`users`) has one identity; they can belong to **businesses** through
**memberships** (client side) and hold **platform roles** (IP Factory side). A **business check** is the lead record
that payments, the full report and the client invitation hang off. The **engagement** starts when the Current State
Assessment is paid and joins the client's business when they create their account (section 2, migration 0008).

## 1. Conventions

- Every table has `id integer generated by default as identity` as its primary key.
- Timestamps are `timestamp with time zone`; `createdAt` defaults to now, and tables that change carry `updatedAt`.
- Tokens are stored as SHA-256 hashes, never in plain text, with one exception noted in section 6. Passwords use scrypt
  with a salt (`hashAdminPassword`, `server/adminSecurity.ts`).
- Enum values are stable identifiers. Labels shown to people come from shared code (`shared/payments.ts`,
  `shared/businessCheck/pipeline.ts`), so a label can change without a migration.
- Schema changes go through `drizzle/schema.ts` then `pnpm db:generate`; never by hand. Migrations `0000` to `0007` are
  applied to Supabase; `0008` (the engagement room) was applied on 10 October (`docs/database-migrations.md`).

### The funnel: check, payments, report, invitation

| Table | What it holds | Keys and rules | Personal or sensitive data |
|---|---|---|---|
| `business_checks` | One free business check: contact, answers, result, summary, pipeline stage, call dates | Unique public token; stage from `PIPELINE_STAGES` | Name, email, WhatsApp, how they heard, business name and description, answers (including revenue band and what the problem costs each month) |
| `business_support_email_deliveries` | Metadata for important Business Support email attempts and their latest provider event | Resend message ID | Recipient and delivery metadata; no email body |
| `resend_email_events` | Deduplicated Resend webhook event metadata | Provider event ID | Event type, provider message ID, recipient and timestamps; no email body |
| `payment_requests` | A payment asked of the owner for the full report, or for a Business Review link sent before F3 (since F3 the Business Review is a fee on its engagement, App flow 3.2), paid on Paystack only | Unique reference (`TS-R-000123`, `TS-CS-000123`); one per check and item; status requested or confirmed (proof received survives only on records from before Paystack only), each move claimed in one conditional statement so a double click (or Paystack's webhook and the return page together) records it once; confirming a report payment issues its `full_reports` row in the same transaction. The 48-hour window runs from `requestedAt`, which resets when the details are sent again, so "48 hours passed" is worked out, not stored. Paystack charges `amountNaira` × 100 kobo, under a reference per attempt that starts with this row's (`TS-R-000123-1a2b3c4d`); no new column holds it (see below) | Amount, reference, the team's note (bank transaction reference). Proof arrives by email and is not stored |
| `full_reports` | The paid report: link, form answers, delivery | One per check; unique token hash; status awaiting_intake or delivered; `reportVersion` | **Financial figures**: revenue, costs, margin, prices, cash, money owed, loans, competitors |
| `client_onboarding_invitations` | The single-use link that creates a client account | Unique token hash; one pending per check; status pending, accepted, revoked, expired; expiry | Email, name and business name at the time |

The full report PDF is **not stored**: it is rebuilt from the stored form answers whenever it is emailed or downloaded,
so the same answers always give the same document.

**How an online payment is recorded** (no schema change). Paystack is a system actor, so a payment it confirms has
`confirmedByUserId` null and `note` "Paid online with Paystack, reference TS-R-000123-1a2b3c4d" ("TEST: " in front when
Paystack ran on its test keys). The `payment_confirmed` and `payment_confirmation_sent` audit rows have a null actor and
`via: "paystack"`, `paystackReference` and `mode` (`test` or `live`) in their details. The client account invitation it
sends needs a person in `client_onboarding_invitations.createdByUserId`: it is the team member who sent the payment
details, and the `client_onboarding_invitation_created` audit row keeps the null actor with `via` and
`recordedAgainstUserId`. A Paystack payment that moved money but could not be confirmed (another amount or currency, or
an item already paid) is recorded as `payment_online_refused`, with the problem, and the office is emailed. Nothing
Paystack sends is stored beyond this: no card details, no customer data.

### The payments ledger (migration 0022, batch F: approved 10 November 2026; F1 built, applied with the ledger code)

What it does and who does it is in the App flow (section 3.1); the code that writes it is `server/ledger.ts`, the
only writer of the payments, the refunds, the counters and a fee's paid state and write-off (F1 and F2 built). Additive: three new tables and five columns on
`engagement_fee_lines`; nothing is dropped. It is applied just before the ledger code ships, not earlier: until then
Save the fees deletes and re-adds every fee, which the ledger's link from a payment to its fee refuses
(`docs/database-migrations.md`).

| Table | What it holds | Keys and rules | Personal or sensitive data |
|---|---|---|---|
| `payments` | One row per attempt to pay and per payment recorded by hand: the fee it pays, or the payment request (the Full Report until R13, and a Business Review link sent before F3); how it came (`paystack`, `bank_transfer`, `cash`, `other`); its status (`started`, `paid`, `failed`, `needs_a_look`, `refunded`, `cancelled`); the amount in whole naira (asked, for an attempt; received, once paid); the Paystack reference and mode (`test`, `live`); the day the money arrived and when it was confirmed or recorded; the receipt number; a note (the bank's reference, or why it needs a look); who recorded it (null for Paystack, a system actor); for a cancelled one, when, who and why | Exactly one of the fee and the payment request is set, and the amount is above nothing (checks `payments_one_owner`, `payments_amount_positive`); unique Paystack reference; unique receipt number (stored as a number, shown as `TS-RC-000001`); a status moves in one conditional statement (`started` to `paid` only where it is still `started`), so the webhook and the return page together record one payment and one receipt; only a payment recorded by hand is cancelled; never deleted; indexed by fee, by payment request and by status | Amounts, references, the team's notes |
| `payment_refunds` | Money returned against one payment: the amount, the day, the reason, Paystack's or the bank's reference, who recorded it; for one recorded by mistake, when, who and why it was cancelled | The amount is above nothing (`payment_refunds_amount_positive`); the refunds of a payment, less cancelled ones, never add up to more than it; refunded in full, the payment's status becomes `refunded`; never deleted | Reasons, references |
| `document_counters` | The last receipt number and the last invoice number given (`receipt`, `invoice`) | One row per kind, added the first time a number is given; a number is taken in the same transaction as the payment or fee it numbers, so numbers are unique, in order and without gaps (a save that fails gives its number back) | — |

New on `engagement_fee_lines`: `invoiceNumber` (unique, stored as a number and shown as `TS-IN-000001`, given the first
time the fee is billed and kept for good) and `writtenOffAmount`, `writtenOffAt`, `writtenOffByUserId` and `writeOffNote` (what was left when a
Partner or the Super Admin wrote it off, when, who and why; cleared when the write-off is reversed, which the audit log
records). `paidAt` stays and now means *paid in full*: set when the fee's payments, less refunds, reach its amount, and
cleared when a refund, a cancellation or a higher amount opens it again. Only the ledger writes it, in the same
transaction as the payment, holding a lock on the fee so two payments cannot both take what is left. `paidReference`
is retired from 0022 (kept so older rows read; the payments hold every reference). A fee's state follows from these:
written off, else paid, else late (dated and past), else due (dated), else set; part paid while something is paid.

**Saving the fees** keeps each fee's id: lines are updated in place, new ones added, and a removed one deleted only if
it was never billed (no invoice number and no payment). A fee's amount cannot go below what is paid on it.

**The backfill**, in 0022's own SQL: every fee marked paid becomes one paid payment for its amount (`paystack` with its
reference when it has one; otherwise `other`, noted "Marked paid before the ledger", and who ticked it stays in the
audit log); every confirmed payment request becomes one paid payment for its amount, with the Paystack reference from
its note and who confirmed it. Receipt numbers go to these in the order they were paid, invoice numbers to the paid fees
in the order they were set, and each counter is left at the last number given. Each statement skips what is already
there, so it can run again. Its guarded apply script is `drizzle/supabase/apply-0022-payments-ledger.sql`.

**Audit actions** (in the audit table below): `fee_payment_recorded`, `fee_payment_cancelled` and `payment_accepted`
(F1); `payment_refund_recorded`, `payment_refund_cancelled`, `fee_written_off` and `fee_write_off_reversed` (F2), each
with the fee or payment, the amount and the reason. `engagement_fee_paid` (the Paid tick) is retired and kept so older
rows read.

**Who sees it:** as for `engagement_fee_lines`, plus Finance (`view_financials`). Recording, cancelling, accepting and
refunds need `manage_payments`; write-offs a new permission, `write_off_fees`, for the Partner role (and the Super
Admin). The owner and full-access staff see each fee's state and what is left, and the receipt number, day and amount
of each payment and refund; never a cancelled one, a note or who recorded it. Never a Specialist. Finance's **Money in**
(F4) reads every payment that counts, with its refunds, the fee or payment request it is for, its receipt number,
Paystack reference and mode, its note and who recorded it, for `view_financials` only; its spreadsheet (CSV) is made in
the browser from what is on screen, so nothing leaves the server that Finance cannot already see.

### Roles and permissions

With batch F (approved 10 November 2026, section 2): a fee payment by hand moved from `manage_engagements` (the Paid
tick) to `manage_payments` (F1); `write_off_fees`, for the partner role, writes off fees and reverses a write-off (F2).

**Privileged access (migration 0012).** Everyone with an internal role signs in with a password and a six-digit code
from an authenticator app. A role is given or removed only by someone whose own highest role ranks above it
(`PLATFORM_ROLE_RANK`: super_admin 100, admin 80, desk_lead and partner 60, finance 50, analyst 40,
subject_matter_expert 20), so only a Super Admin can make an Admin. A specialist's role ends on a date. **Remove access**
(People, `manage_users`) takes every role, engagement, session and the two-step set-up in one step, suspends the
account and records the reason. The Super Admins are emailed (and the audit log records `security_alert`) for a role
given, a staff invitation, a sign-in from a new device, a locked staff account, five wrong codes, a recovery code used,
a two-step reset and access removed. The controls against the NIST Cybersecurity Framework: `docs/security/nist-csf.md`.

Business membership roles (`shared/businessCapabilities.ts`): an **owner** can view, edit the profile, manage members,
invite and manage billing; a **business_admin** can view, edit and manage members; a **member** can view.

### How client isolation is enforced

`server/accountAuth.ts`:

- `resolveAccountSession` checks the session against the database on every call (hash, not revoked, not expired,
  person active).
- `loadMemberships` returns only active memberships in active businesses; `chooseActiveBusiness` keeps the stored
  workspace only while it is still a membership.
- `requireBusinessMembership` never trusts a business id sent by the browser; an unknown business and someone else's
  business get the same refusal.
- `requireBusinessCapability` adds the membership role check.
- A client's session never becomes a staff identity: the context builds a staff user only for a person with a platform
  role (`server/_core/context.ts`).

## 5. How the engagement room decides access

1. **The team: permission and assignment.** Seeing an engagement needs `view_all_businesses`, or
   `view_assigned_businesses` and a row in `engagement_team`. Changing it also needs `manage_engagements`;
   changing the team needs `assign_engagements`; approving a prescription or plan needs `review_engagements`. An
   engagement outside someone's scope and one that does not exist get the same "not available".
2. **The client: membership and audience.** The room shows the engagement of the business the person is working in;
   no client procedure accepts a business id. Each note, deliverable and file carries an audience: `team` (never
   leaves IP Factory), `owner` (default for notes and findings, because owners speak frankly on our calls) or
   `business` (the owner's staff with full access). Contributors see only the requests and actions given to them.
   Only owner-level people sign off or change an audience.
3. **"Shared" is stored** with who and when, and every share, approval, sign-off and audience change is in the audit
   trail, so "when did the client see this?" has an answer.
4. **Finance** sees payments, not engagements: no finance permission opens the room.

5. **Invitations** (`account_invitations`, `server/accountInvitations.ts`): a staff invitation creates the person with
   one role; a seat invitation creates the person with a membership and an access level, in the owner's business only,
   within the included seat. Accepting is one transaction; an existing account is never merged.
